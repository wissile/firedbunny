Fact-checked October 21, 2026. Format: figure, tax year, source. "UNVERIFIED" means the figure could not be confirmed from a fetched source this session. "(computed)" means it was derived arithmetically from confirmed figures.

## 1. Retirement plans (2026). Primary source: IRS Notice 2025-67 / IR-2025-111 (Nov 13, 2025)
Sources: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf and https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500

- 401(k)/403(b)/gov't 457/TSP elective deferral, §402(g): **$24,500** (2025: $23,500). Source: N-25-67
- Age 50+ catch-up, §414(v): **$8,000** (2025: $7,500). Source: N-25-67
- Age 60–63 "super catch-up": **$11,250** (unchanged; replaces the $8,000). Max total deferral at ages 60–63: $35,750 (computed). Source: N-25-67
- Total annual additions, §415(c) (employee + employer + after-tax): **$72,000** (2025: $70,000). Source: N-25-67
- Compensation limit, §401(a)(17): **$360,000** (2025: $350,000). Source: N-25-67
- HCE threshold, §414(q): **$160,000** (unchanged). Key employee, §416: $235,000. Source: N-25-67
- Defined benefit limit, §415(b): $290,000. Source: N-25-67
- Mandatory Roth catch-up (SECURE 2.0 §603, IRC §414(v)(7)):
  - 2026 wage threshold: **$150,000** (2025: $145,000). Source: N-25-67
  - Test: FICA wages (§3121(a), W-2 box 3) from the plan sponsor in the **preceding** calendar year. Per statute. The threshold-to-year mapping (2025 wages > $150,000 means Roth catch-up in 2026) is the common practitioner reading: UNVERIFIED in an IRS text this session.
  - Timing: the administrative transition (Notice 2023-62) ended **Dec 31, 2025**, so the rule is in force for 2026 under good-faith compliance. Final regs (IR-2025-91, Sep 15, 2025) generally apply to tax years beginning after **Dec 31, 2026** (later for some governmental and collectively bargained plans). https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-issue-final-regulations-on-new-roth-catch-up-rule-other-secure-2point0-act-provisions
- IRA contribution limit: **$7,500** (2025: $7,000). IRA catch-up (50+): **$1,100** (2025: $1,000). Source: N-25-67
- Roth IRA MAGI phase-outs, 2026. Source: N-25-67
  - Single/HOH: **$153,000–$168,000**
  - MFJ: **$242,000–$252,000**
  - MFS: **$0–$10,000** (not indexed)
- Traditional IRA deduction phase-outs, 2026. Source: N-25-67
  - Single/HOH, covered by a workplace plan: **$81,000–$91,000**
  - MFJ, contributor covered: **$129,000–$149,000**
  - Not covered but spouse covered (MFJ): **$242,000–$252,000**
  - MFS, covered: $0–$10,000
- SIMPLE IRA/401(k), 2026. Source: N-25-67
  - Deferral: **$17,000** ($18,100 for certain "applicable" SIMPLE plans)
  - 50+ catch-up: **$4,000** ($3,850 for applicable plans)
  - 60–63 catch-up: **$5,250**
  - Extra nonelective cap: $5,300
- SEP IRA, 2026: employer contribution is the lesser of 25% of compensation or **$72,000** (§415(c)). Compensation cap is $360,000 (§408(k)(3)(C)). Minimum compensation to be covered: **$800**. Source: N-25-67
- Saver's Credit AGI tiers, 2026. Rates are 50% / 20% / 10%; 0% above the top tier. Max eligible contribution $2,000 ($4,000 MFJ); max credit $1,000 ($2,000 MFJ). Tiers from N-25-67; rate structure from https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-savings-contributions-savers-credit
  - MFJ: 50% ≤ $48,500 · 20% ≤ $52,500 · 10% ≤ **$80,500**
  - HOH: 50% ≤ $36,375 · 20% ≤ $39,375 · 10% ≤ **$60,375**
  - Single/MFS: 50% ≤ $24,250 · 20% ≤ $26,250 · 10% ≤ **$40,250**
- Other 2026 limits: QLAC premium limit $210,000. PLESA (pension-linked emergency savings account) $2,600. Source: N-25-67
- 2027 401(k)/IRA limits: NOT announced as of Oct 21, 2026. Projections only; do not publish as official.

## 2. HSA / FSA / commuter
- HSA limits, 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-19): self-only **$4,400**, family **$8,750**. https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-19.pdf
- HSA age-55+ catch-up: **$1,000**, a fixed amount with no indexing stated. https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969
- HDHP, 2026: minimum deductible **$1,700** self / **$3,400** family. Max out-of-pocket **$8,500** self / **$17,000** family. Excepted-benefit HRA $2,200. Source: RP-25-19
- **2027 (announced, Rev. Proc. 2026-24):** https://www.ifebp.org/detail-pages/news/2026/06/01/irs-releases-2027-hsa--hdhp--and-hra-inflation-adjusted-amounts
  - HSA: $4,500 self / $9,000 family
  - HDHP minimum deductible: $1,750 / $3,500
  - HDHP max out-of-pocket: $8,700 / $17,400
  - Excepted-benefit HRA: $2,250
- OBBBA HSA changes (IRS Notice 2026-5, Dec 9, 2025). Sources: https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/12/notice-2026-5-expanded-hsa-eligibility-under-obbba.html and https://rsmus.com/insights/services/business-tax/irs-notice-2026-expanded-hdhp-definition-obbba-broadens-hsa.html
  - Telehealth: first-dollar telehealth no longer blocks HSA eligibility, made **permanent**, for plan years beginning on/after **Jan 1, 2025**. Pub 969 confirms. https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969
  - Bronze and catastrophic plans: treated as HSA-compatible from **Jan 1, 2026**. The plan need not be bought on an Exchange.
  - Direct primary care: from **Jan 1, 2026**, DPC enrollees can still contribute to an HSA, and HSA money can pay DPC fees tax-free. Fee cap is **$150/mo individual, $300/mo family** (per RSM; whether the cap is indexed: UNVERIFIED).
- Health FSA, 2026: **$3,400** salary reduction limit. Max carryover **$680** (if the plan allows). Rev. Proc. 2025-32 / IR-2025-103. https://www.irs.gov/node/151941
- Dependent care FSA (§129): **$7,500** ($3,750 MFS) from **Jan 1, 2026**, up from $5,000. Set by OBBBA; first increase since 1986; employer must adopt. Indexing: none reported (UNVERIFIED). https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/new-year-new-dependent-care-fsa-limits
- Child & dependent care credit, from 2026: max credit rate rises from 35% to **50%** (OBBBA §70405). Expense caps stay **$3,000 / $6,000**. https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-provisions
- Commuter benefits, 2026: transit/vanpool **$340/mo** and qualified parking **$340/mo**. Source: IR-2025-103 https://www.irs.gov/node/151941

- Standard deduction, 2026: single/MFS **$16,100**, MFJ **$32,200**, HOH **$24,150**. 2025 (as amended by OBBBA): $15,750 / $31,500 / $23,625. Source: RP-25-32
- Additional standard deduction (65+ or blind), 2026: **$1,650** per condition if married; **$2,050** if unmarried, not a surviving spouse. Source: RP-25-32 §4.14(3)
- Dependent standard deduction, 2026: greater of $1,350 or earned income + $450. Kiddie tax threshold $1,350. Source: RP-25-32
- AMT exemption, 2026: $90,100 unmarried / $140,200 MFJ. Phase-out starts $500,000 / $1,000,000. Source: IR-2025-103
- **Senior "bonus" deduction** (OBBBA): https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-tax-deductions-for-working-americans-and-seniors
  - Amount and years: **$6,000** per person aged 65+ ($12,000 if both spouses qualify), tax years **2025–2028**
  - Phase-out: starts at MAGI **$75,000** single / **$150,000** MFJ; reduced by **6%** of MAGI above the threshold (rate per NATP: https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/new-client-with-obbba-deductions-review-before-you-roll-forward/)
  - Requirements: SSN on the return; married couples must file jointly; available to itemizers and non-itemizers
- **No tax on tips**: max **$25,000**/yr, **2025–2028**, above the line. Source: IRS FS above
  - Phase-out: starts at MAGI $150,000 single / $300,000 MFJ; reduced $100 per $1,000 above (Buchalter)
  - Fully phased out at $400,000 single / $550,000 MFJ (computed)
  - Eligibility: occupations on the IRS "customarily tipped" list; SSTBs excluded; SSN required; married couples must file jointly
- **No tax on overtime** (FLSA premium portion only): max **$12,500** single / **$25,000** MFJ, **2025–2028**. Source: IRS FS
  - Phase-out: starts at $150,000 / $300,000; reduced $100 per $1,000 above (NATP)
- **Car loan interest**: max **$10,000**/yr, **2025–2028**. Source: IRS FS
  - Loan: originated after **Dec 31, 2024** and secured by the vehicle
  - Vehicle: **new** (original use), personal use, under 14,000 lb GVWR, **final assembly in the US**; VIN required on the return; leases and used vehicles do not qualify
  - Phase-out: starts at MAGI $100,000 single / $200,000 MFJ; reduced $200 per $1,000 above (NATP). Fully phased out at $150,000 / $250,000 (computed)
- **SALT cap**:
  - 2025: **$40,000** ($20,000 MFS). Reduced by 30% of MAGI above **$500,000** ($250,000 MFS, **not** "other filers"), but never below **$10,000** ($5,000 MFS). Source: 2025 Schedule A instructions https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sca
  - 2026: **$40,400** cap (CRS IN12686, https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-04-30_IN12686_7da5b9939a3ca563351fcac235198a67292933a2.pdf)
  - 2026 phase-down threshold: **$505,000** ($252,500 MFS). Cap reaches the $10,000 floor at about $606,333 MAGI (computed). https://q3adv.com/salt-deduction-cap-2026/
  - Later years: cap and threshold rise 1%/yr through 2029 (2027 cap $40,804, computed). Cap reverts to **$10,000 in 2030**. Source: Buchalter https://www.buchalter.com/insights/key-tax-changes-in-public-law-119-21-what-individuals-and-businesses-need-to-know/
- Charitable deductions:
  - Non-itemizers, from **2026**: up to **$1,000** single / **$2,000** MFJ of **cash** gifts. Not allowed for donor-advised funds, supporting organizations, noncash gifts, or carryovers. https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-provisions
  - Itemizers, from **2026**: only gifts above **0.5% of AGI** are deductible. The 60%-of-AGI cash limit is made permanent. Source: Buchalter
- Top-bracket limit on itemized deductions, from **2026**: tax value capped at 35¢ per $1. Mechanism: deductions reduced by 2/37 of the lesser of itemized deductions or taxable income above the 37% bracket threshold. Source: CRS IN12686
- Mortgage interest: deductible on **$750,000** of acquisition debt ($375,000 MFS) for debt taken out after Dec 15, 2017; older debt keeps the $1M limit. Source: Sched A instructions
  - OBBBA made the $750k limit permanent; home-equity interest remains non-deductible unless the loan is used to buy, build or improve the home (Buchalter)
- **PMI** (mortgage insurance premiums): deductible again as mortgage interest from **2026**, permanently. Source: Buchalter (not confirmed on irs.gov this session)
- Child Tax Credit, 2026: **$2,200** per child, refundable portion (ACTC) **$1,700**. Source: RP-25-32 §4.05
  - Phase-out begins at $200,000 / $400,000 MFJ (not indexed)
  - SSN required for the taxpayer and the child (Buchalter)
- **Trump Accounts** (§530A; OBBBA §70204). Sources: IR-2026-42 (Mar 31, 2026) https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/4-million-children-have-been-signed-up-for-trump-accounts-with-1-million-claiming-the-1000-pilot-program-contribution and https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-provisions
  - Federal pilot deposit: one-time **$1,000** for US-citizen children with an SSN born **Jan 1, 2025 – Dec 31, 2028**
  - Who can have an account: any child under 18 with an SSN. Elected on **Form 4547**
  - Contributions: began **July 4, 2026**. Annual limit **$5,000** from all sources, indexed after 2027 per Buchalter
  - Employer: up to **$2,500**/yr, excluded from the employee's income
  - Investments: US-stock index funds/ETFs. Generally no withdrawals before 18; IRA-like rules after that
- 529 plans (OBBBA). Source: Buchalter
  - K-12 tuition limit raised from $10,000 to **$20,000/yr** (2026+)
  - K-12 expenses broadened: curriculum, books/materials, online courses, tutoring, test fees, dual enrollment, educational therapies
  - Post-secondary credentialing/licensing costs qualify for distributions after Jul 4, 2025
  - 529-to-ABLE rollovers made permanent
- Estate and gift tax, 2026. Source: RP-25-32 / IR-2025-103
  - Basic exclusion: **$15,000,000** (2025: $13,990,000). OBBBA makes it permanent and indexed
  - Annual gift exclusion: **$19,000**
  - Gifts to a non-citizen spouse: **$194,000**
- Adoption credit, 2026: $17,670, of which $5,120 is refundable. Source: IR-2025-103

  - The thresholds are **not indexed** for inflation (IRS Q&A). https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/questions-and-answers-on-the-net-investment-income-tax
- Qualified dividends: taxed at the LTCG rates above. Source: https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc404
  - Holding period: more than 60 days in the 121-day window around the ex-dividend date (90 of 181 days for preferred). UNVERIFIED this session (§1(h)(11); Pub 550 text was truncated)
- Capital loss deduction against ordinary income: **$3,000/yr** ($1,500 MFS); unused losses carry forward indefinitely. Source: Topic 409
- Collectibles: max **28%**. §1202 QSBS taxable portion: 28%. Unrecaptured §1250 gain: max **25%**. Short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income. Holding period for long-term: more than 1 year. Source: Topic 409
- QSBS (§1202), OBBBA, for stock issued after Jul 4, 2025. Source: Buchalter
  - Exclusion cap: $15M (indexed)
  - Tiered exclusion: 50% after 3 years, 75% after 4 years, 100% after 5 years
  - Gross-asset limit: $75M

## 5. Social Security
- COLA: **2.8% for 2026**; **3.6% for 2027**, announced Oct 14, 2026 (the largest since 2023). Sources: https://www.ssa.gov/blog/en/posts/2026-10-14.html and https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/index.html
- Taxable wage maximum: **$184,500** (2026); **$192,300** (2027). 2025 was $176,100. https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2027.html
- Earnings test. Sources: SSA 2026 and 2027 fact sheets (https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html)
  - Under FRA all year: **$24,480/yr** ($2,040/mo) in 2026; **$25,560** in 2027. SSA withholds $1 per $2 over the limit
  - Year reaching FRA (counts only months before FRA): **$65,160** ($5,430/mo) in 2026; **$67,920** in 2027. SSA withholds $1 per $3 over the limit
- Full retirement age: born 1943–54 = 66; born 1955–59 = 66 + 2 to 10 months; born **1960 or later = 67**. https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html
- Claiming at 62 with FRA 67: worker benefit cut **30%**, spousal benefit cut **35%**. Formula: 5/9 of 1% per month for the first 36 months early, then 5/12 of 1% per month. Source: SSA agereduction page
- Delayed retirement credits (born 1943+): **8%/yr** (2/3 of 1% per month) until age 70. +24% at 70 if FRA is 67 (computed). https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/delayret.html
- Maximum benefit:
  - At FRA: **$4,152/mo** (2026); **$4,188/mo** (2027). Source: SSA 2027 fact sheet
  - At age 62 (2026): **$2,969**. Source: Motley Fool, Oct 3, 2026 https://www.fool.com/retirement/2026/10/03/heres-the-max-social-security-benefit-at-age-62/
  - At age 70 (2026): **CONFLICT, UNVERIFIED**. Motley Fool says $5,181; Nasdaq/Motley Fool syndication says $5,251 (https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/new-max-social-security-benefit-2026). The SSA source page (https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/examplemax.html) was blocked this session; check it before publishing.
- Credits: **40** needed; max 4/yr. Earnings per credit: **$1,890** (2026), **$1,970** (2027). Sources: https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/credits.html and SSA 2027 fact sheet
- Other 2026 → 2027 figures (SSA 2027 fact sheet):
  - SSI federal payment: $994 → $1,029 individual; $1,491 → $1,544 couple
  - SGA: $1,690 → $1,770 non-blind; $2,830 → $2,950 blind
  - Average retired-worker benefit: about $2,071 (Jan 2026); about $2,170 after the 2027 COLA
- Taxation of benefits. Provisional ("combined") income = AGI + tax-exempt interest + ½ of benefits. Thresholds are not indexed (statutory since 1983/1993; Pub 915 shows no indexing). Source: Pub 915 (2025) https://www.irs.gov/publications/p915
  - Single/HOH: up to 50% taxable above **$25,000**; up to 85% above **$34,000**
  - MFJ: up to 50% above **$32,000**; up to 85% above **$44,000**
  - MFS living together: base amount $0
- Social Security Fairness Act: signed **Jan 5, 2025**. Repealed WEP and GPO for benefits payable from Jan 2024. About 2.8M people affected; 3.1M+ retroactive payments ($17B) sent by Jul 7, 2025. https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/social-security-fairness-act.html
- 2026 Trustees Report (SSA page dated Jun 2026). Sources: https://www.ssa.gov/OACT/TR/2026/II_E_conclu.html and https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13256.html
  - OASI depleted in **2032** (Q4); 78% of benefits payable after that
  - Combined OASDI depleted in **2034** (Q3); about 83% payable after that
  - DI not depleted within 75 years

- Inherited IRA 10-year rule (owner died after 2019). Sources: IRS RMD FAQ and Pub 590-B https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b
  - Non-eligible designated beneficiaries must empty the account by Dec 31 of the 10th year after death
  - If the owner died on or after the required beginning date, annual RMDs are also required in years 1–9
  - Exempt beneficiaries: spouse, minor child, disabled or chronically ill person, and anyone not more than 10 years younger than the owner
