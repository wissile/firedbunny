### Cost buckets of owning
- **Mortgage interest**: largest early cost (CALC: year-1 interest on a $400k loan at 7.4% ≈ $29.5k; principal only ≈ $3.8k).
- **Property tax**: ~1%/yr is the 5%-rule assumption, but rates vary widely by state. A sourced national effective rate is UNVERIFIED.
- **Maintenance**: ~**1% of value/yr** (PWL 5% rule). https://pwlcapital.com/?p=8502
- **Homeowners insurance**: rising, especially in FL, CA, and LA. A sourced 2026 national average is UNVERIFIED.
- **HOA**: varies, $0 to $1,000+/mo. UNVERIFIED.
- **PMI** if less than 20% down (see §6).
- **Closing costs to buy**: typically **2%–5%** (some sources say of the loan, some of the price). ClosingCorp average ≈ **$6,800** for a single-family home. https://themortgagereports.com/35800/guide-to-mortgage-closing-costs-what-average-mortgage-costs-are-and-how-to-keep-yours-low
- **Costs to sell**: agent commissions historically **5%–6%**, typically paid by the seller (same source). After the NAR settlement (effective **Aug 17, 2024**), the average buyer-agent commission was 2.36% in Q3 2024, 2.43% in Q2 2025, and **2.42% in Q3 2025** (Redfin), so commissions are largely unchanged. https://nowbam.com/buyer-agent-commissions-rise-to-2-43-one-year-after-nar-settlement/ ; https://themortgagepoint.com/2025/12/31/buyers-agent-commissions-inch-up-as-slower-market-shifts-leverage-to-buyers/
  - An all-in selling cost of **~6%–10%** (commissions + transfer taxes + title + concessions + prep) is a common estimate. UNVERIFIED as a range.
- **Opportunity cost of the down payment**: cash in the house isn't earning market returns. This is part of the 5% rule's "cost of capital."

### The 5% rule (Ben Felix / PWL Capital)
- Unrecoverable cost of owning ≈ **5% of home value/yr** = 1% property tax + 1% maintenance + 3% cost of capital (mortgage interest plus the opportunity cost of equity, using a ~3% stock-vs-real-estate return gap). https://pwlcapital.com/?p=8502
- Use: value × 5% ÷ 12 vs. monthly rent. If rent is lower, renting is financially competitive. Inverse: rent × 12 ÷ 5% = equivalent home price. PWL example: $500k home ≈ $2,083/mo; $3,000 rent ≈ owning a $720k home. PWL says ~4% may fit conservative or taxable-heavy investors. (Same link.)
- CALC at national medians: $417,500 × 5% / 12 ≈ **$1,740/mo** vs typical rent $1,932, so the median roughly favors owning on paper over a long hold. Equivalently, price/annual rent of ~18 is below the 5% rule's 20× breakeven. Caveat (judgment, UNVERIFIED): Felix's 3% capital-cost term was calibrated when mortgages were cheaper. At 7.4% rates the cost-of-capital term is arguably higher, so check local numbers.

### Break-even horizon / when buying makes sense
- Transaction costs alone (CALC: ~3% to buy + ~6% to sell ≈ 9% of price) take **~2+ years of 4% appreciation** just to recover. Most rent-vs-buy calculators therefore show break-evens of **~5+ years**. The "5+ years" figure is a common rule. UNVERIFIED as a specific statistic.
- An Atlanta Fed review notes other studies find owners holding ~7 years pay ~44% less than renters. Shiller-based housing price returns "hover around zero" in real terms. https://www.atlantafed.org/blogs/real-estate-research/2013/06/12/is-investing-in-housing-really-a-losing-proposition
- Buying tends to win with: a long expected stay (5–7+ years), stable job and income, a local price-to-rent below ~15–20×, cash for the down payment plus closing costs plus a reserve, and a fixed-rate loan with a payment comfortably inside DTI limits. Renting tends to win with: a short horizon, likely relocation, high price-to-rent (>20×), job uncertainty, or no emergency fund. (Synthesis of the sources above. Thresholds are rules of thumb.)

## 2. Primary-Home Taxes

- **§121 exclusion**: up to **$250,000** of gain ($500,000 MFJ). You must have owned **and** lived in the home ≥24 months (730 days) in the 5 years before sale. The months don't need to be continuous. For MFJ, only one spouse needs to meet ownership, but both must meet use. The exclusion can be used only **once per 2-year period**. IRS Pub 523: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p523
  - **Partial exclusion** if the main reason for selling is a change of workplace, health, or an unforeseeable event. Take the shortest of (use, ownership, time since last exclusion) ÷ 24 months × $250k. Example (CALC): a single filer who lived there 12 months and moved for work gets 12/24 × $250k = **$125k**. (Pub 523)
  - **Nonqualified use** after 2008 (periods when it wasn't your main home, e.g., a rental before you moved in) reduces the excludable gain. Depreciation allowed after May 6, 1997 **can't be excluded**. (Pub 523)
- **Mortgage interest**: acquisition debt up to **$750,000** ($375,000 MFS) for debt after Dec 15, 2017. Earlier debt keeps $1M ($500k MFS). Applies to a main home plus one second home, itemizers only. https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936
  - **OBBBA made the $750k cap permanent.** It would otherwise have reverted to $1M after 2025. The cap is not inflation-indexed. https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/trumps-big-beautiful-bill-locks-in-key-tax-breaks-for-homeowners-heres-what-to-know/3865850/?amp=1
  - **Home equity loan/HELOC interest** is deductible only if the funds are used to **buy, build, or substantially improve** the home securing the loan (Pub 936). Debt consolidation or a car → not deductible.
- **Mortgage insurance premiums (PMI, FHA MIP, VA funding fee, USDA fee)**: **deductible again starting tax year 2026**, permanently, treated as mortgage interest. Itemizers only. The deduction phases out above **$100,000 AGI** ($50,000 MFS) and is gone around $109k. It was not deductible for 2022–2025 (Pub 936 for 2025 says "expired"). https://mortgage.archgroup.com/the-65-billion-tax-break-that-just-changed-everything-for-homebuyers/ ; NBC link above. The exact phase-out mechanics (historically 10% per $1,000 over the threshold) are UNVERIFIED for the new law.
- **SALT cap (state/local income + property taxes)**:
  - 2025: $40,000 with a $500k MAGI threshold.
  - **2026: $40,400** ($20,200 MFS). The cap is reduced by **30% of MAGI above $505,000** ($252,500 MFS) but not below **$10,000** ($5,000 MFS).
  - Cap and threshold rise 1%/yr through 2029, then revert to $10,000 in 2030. https://www.irs.gov/node/156811 ; https://kiplinger.com/taxes/salt-deduction-gets-an-update-for-2026-taxes ; https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/?p=167509
  - Example (CALC): MAGI $555,000 → $40,400 − 30%×$50,000 = **$25,400** cap.
- **Standard deduction 2026**: **$16,100** single/MFS, **$32,200** MFJ, $24,150 HOH. https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill
  - Only about **10%** of households itemize after TCJA, vs ~30% before (TPC estimate via Kitces; exact year not given). https://www.kitces.com/blog/itemized-deductions-after-tcja-sustaining-intermittent-add-on-standard-deduction-threshold/
  - Example (CALC): an MFJ couple with $14k mortgage interest + $8k property tax = $22k is still under $32,200, so the mortgage gives **no tax benefit**.
- **Basis records**: basis = purchase price + certain purchase costs + **capital improvements** (not repairs). Keep receipts for as long as you own the home plus the limitations period after sale. (Pub 523)
- **Step-up at death**: an heir's basis = FMV at date of death (or the alternate valuation date). In community-property states, **both halves** generally step up. Exception: appreciated property gifted to the decedent within 1 year of death and returned to the donor or spouse. IRS Pub 551: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551

## 3. Housing as an Investment

- Shiller real (inflation-adjusted) U.S. home prices:
  - 1890–2022: up ~122% total, **~0.6%/yr above inflation**.
  - 1890–1989: <0.3%/yr.
  - 1989–2022: >1.6%/yr.
  - Stocks: ~6–7%/yr real (source gives no period).
  https://awealthofcommonsense.com/2022/12/is-real-estate-a-better-investment-than-the-stock-market/
- Atlanta Fed: a Shiller-based housing return weighted average of **0.97%** vs **4.55%** for the S&P 500, which excludes imputed rent. https://www.atlantafed.org/blogs/real-estate-research/2013/06/12/is-investing-in-housing-really-a-losing-proposition
- Price indices exclude the main return of owning, which is **imputed rent** (not paying rent), and also exclude costs (taxes, maintenance, transaction costs).
- **Leverage example (CALC)**: $400k home, 20% down ($80k).
  - +4% appreciation = $16k = **+20% on equity** before costs.
  - 0% appreciation = 0%.
  - −10% appreciation = **−50% of equity**.
  - Over 5 yrs at 4%/yr: value $486.7k, minus 6% selling ($29.2k), minus loan balance ($302.5k) = $155k net proceeds vs $92k invested (down payment + 3% closing) → +$63k. But 5-yr interest at 7.4% ≈ $115k, and taxes + maintenance + insurance add ≈ $50k more. The math works only once you credit the rent you avoided.
- Risks: illiquidity (selling takes months and ~6–10% in costs), concentration (one asset in one local market, often correlated with your job), and leverage that magnifies losses (AWOCS cites concentration, illiquidity, landlord headaches; link above).
- **House hacking**:
  - FHA allows **1–4 units** with **3.5% down at a 580+ score** if you occupy one unit as your primary residence for at least 1 year.
  - Lenders typically count ~**75%** of the other units' rent toward qualifying.
  - **3–4 unit** FHA loans must pass the **self-sufficiency test**: net market rents after a vacancy factor must cover the full PITI + MIP. Duplexes are exempt.
  - Buying without intent to occupy is occupancy fraud.
  https://www.mortgagedaily.com/rates/strategies/house-hacking-fha-multi-unit/
  - Conventional: Fannie Mae cut the down payment for owner-occupied 2–4 unit homes to **5%** (from 15–25%) starting in mid-Nov 2023. https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/fannie-mae-slashes-down-payment-requirements-multifamily-homes-5
- **HELOC basics**:
  - Borrows against equity, usually at a **variable rate**.
  - A **draw period** is followed by a **repayment period**, and payments are "often significantly higher" in repayment. Some HELOCs require a balloon payment.
  - The lender can **freeze or reduce** the line if your finances or the home's value drop.
  - Default can mean **losing the home**.
  https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc-en-107/

## 4. Long-Term Rental Property

### Income/expense model and metrics
- Expenses: mortgage interest, property tax, insurance (landlord/"dwelling fire" policy, pricier than HO-3; UNVERIFIED), repairs, **management 8%–12% of collected rent** (≈10% typical) plus a leasing fee of ~$300–$500 (https://ipropertymanagement.com/guides/property-management-fees), **vacancy 5%–10%** (rule of thumb, UNVERIFIED), and a capex reserve of ~5%–10% of rent (rule of thumb, UNVERIFIED).
- **Cap rate** = NOI ÷ price, where NOI excludes the mortgage. **Cash-on-cash** = annual pre-tax cash flow after debt service ÷ cash invested (standard definitions).
- **1% rule**: monthly rent ≥ 1% of price (+ rehab). It is a screening filter, not a rule. BiggerPockets (Dave Meyer) calls it an "outdated suggestion" from the post-2008 market, since it ignores rates, taxes, and insurance. https://www.biggerpockets.com/blog/1-percent-rule-dead
  - National context (CALC): $1,932 / $366,913 ≈ **0.53%/mo**, about half the 1% target.
- **Example (CALC, illustrative)**: $300k house, rent $2,100/mo (0.7%).
  - Gross $25,200. Minus 7% vacancy ($1,764), 10% management ($2,344), 1.1% property tax ($3,300), insurance ($1,800), 5% repairs ($1,260), 5% capex ($1,260).
  - **NOI $13,472 → cap rate 4.5%.**
  - With 25% down ($75k + $9k closing) at 7.4%, debt service is $18,694, so **cash flow ≈ −$5,200/yr (CoC −6%)**.
  - Lesson: at 7%+ rates, many leveraged rentals don't cash-flow. Returns then rely on appreciation, principal paydown, and tax shelter.
  - Investment-property rates usually run above owner-occupied rates. UNVERIFIED by how much.

### Tax rules
- **Depreciation**: residential rental buildings are depreciated over **27.5 years**, straight-line (MACRS GDS). **Land is not depreciable.** IRS Pub 527: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
  - Mid-month convention: standard per Pub 946, not re-fetched. UNVERIFIED this session.
  - Example (CALC): $300k with 80% building → $240k / 27.5 = **$8,727/yr**.
- **Recapture on sale**: depreciation on real property ("unrecaptured §1250 gain") is taxed at a **maximum 25%**. https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409
  - Example (CALC): 10 years × $8,727 = $87,273 → up to **$21,818** tax.
  - Cost-segregated §1245 components are recaptured at **ordinary** rates. https://www.redawning.com/pm/post/short-term-rental-tax-loophole-2026
  - NIIT 3.8% may also apply. The rate itself is UNVERIFIED in fetched text; Topic 409 points to Topic 559.
- **Passive activity rules** (IRS Pub 925, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925):
  - Rental real estate is passive by default.
  - **$25,000 special allowance** against nonpassive income for **active participation**. It is reduced by **50% of MAGI over $100,000** and **gone at $150,000** ($50k–$75k for MFS living apart).
  - Example (CALC): MAGI $120k → $25k − 50%×$20k = **$15k** allowance.
  - Disallowed losses carry forward and are released on a fully taxable sale. Report on Form 8582.
- **Real estate professional (REPS)**: **more than half** of your personal services in trades or businesses go to real property trades or businesses in which you materially participate, **and more than 750 hours** in them. You must also materially participate in the rentals. (Pub 925) A full-time W-2 employee rarely qualifies.
- **1031 exchange** (Form 8824 instructions, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8824.pdf):
  - **Real property only** since 2018, held for business or investment. Not for property held primarily for sale, and not for a primary residence.
  - **Identify** the replacement property in writing within **45 days**. **Receive** it by the earlier of **180 days** or the return due date (with extensions).
  - Deferred exchanges use a **qualified intermediary**. Cash or other "boot" is taxable to the extent of gain.
  - Related-party 2-year rule applies.
- **Bonus depreciation / cost segregation (OBBBA)**:
  - **100% bonus depreciation restored permanently** for qualified property **acquired and placed in service after Jan 19, 2025**. Property acquired earlier stays on the phase-down (40% in 2025). (Pub 527; Thomson Reuters on **Notice 2026-11**, Jan 14, 2026: https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/irs-provides-guidance-on-post-obbb-bonus-depreciation/) The 20% for 2026 on pre-Jan-20-2025 property is per RedAwning (link above).
  - Bonus applies only to ≤20-year property, so **the building itself is excluded**. Cost segregation reclassifies typically **20%–35%** of basis into 5/7/15-year property. https://beancount.io/blog/2026/06/05/short-term-rental-str-loophole-material-participation-tax-strategy-airbnb-vrbo-hosts-obbba-100-bonus-depreciation-cost-segregation-section-469-seven-day-rule-w2-offset-guide
  - For long-term rentals, a big bonus-driven loss is still passive unless the owner has REPS or the loss falls within the $25k allowance.
- **Reporting**: rentals go on **Schedule E**. If you provide **significant services** (e.g., maid service), report on **Schedule C**. Heat, light, and trash don't count. Spouses who co-own through an LLC can't use the QJV election. https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se
- **LLC basics** (UNVERIFIED this session): an LLC gives liability separation but doesn't change federal tax for a single-member LLC (disregarded entity). Deeding a mortgaged property into an LLC can trigger the loan's due-on-sale clause. Umbrella insurance is a common complement or alternative.

## 5. Short-Term Rental (Airbnb/Vrbo)

### Platform fees (2026)
- **Airbnb host-only ("single") fee**:
  - Most hosts pay **15.5%** of the booking subtotal, which **includes cleaning, pet, and extra-guest fees** but excludes taxes. Brazil and Mexico pay 16%. Others typically pay 14–16%. Guests pay **no separate Airbnb service fee**.
  - Once switched, a host can't switch back.
  https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1857 ; https://www.hostfully.com/blog/airbnb-host-fees/
- **Legacy split fee**: host **3%** (4% in Brazil/Mexico) + guest **14.1%–16.5%**. (Airbnb help, link above)
- **Rollout**:
  - Announced Sept 2025 for software/PMS-connected hosts (https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/airbnb-single-service-fee/).
  - Mandatory for US/Canada PMS-connected hosts on **Oct 27, 2025**. Simplified pricing rose from 15% to 15.5% on Dec 1, 2025.
  - Independent non-EU hosts moved **~Sept 15, 2026**. EU/EEA hosts are moving ~Oct 13, 2026.
  - Sources: Hostfully (above); https://www.smoobu.com/en/blog/airbnb-host-only-fee-increase/ ; https://www.hostaway.com/blog/airbnb-service-fee-change/. Hospitable says Sept 17, 2026, a minor date discrepancy. Airbnb's own page gives no dates.
  - **So by Oct 2026, essentially all U.S. hosts pay ~15.5% host-only.** To net the same as before, a host must mark prices up ~18.3% (1/0.845 − 1) to offset 15.5%. https://help.hospitable.com/en/articles/4703232
- **Vrbo**:
  - Pay-per-booking was **5% commission** (on rent + host fees, excluding taxes) **+ 3% payment processing**, about 8% (~5% if PMS-connected). Annual subscription ($499–$699) is closed to new hosts. Travelers pay **6%–15%**. https://www.hostfully.com/blog/vrbo-host-fees/
  - **New: flat 12% commission for all hosts effective Oct 29, 2026** (announced Sept 29, 2026), replacing the 5%+3% model. https://www.smoobu.com/en/?p=82687 This comes from a single secondary source; verify on Vrbo before publishing.

### Operations and market data
- **Occupancy/lodging taxes**: Airbnb **automatically collects and remits certain taxes** in some jurisdictions, based on the listing address. Hosts remain responsible for any taxes Airbnb doesn't collect. https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/654/what-is-occupancy-tax--do-i-need-to-collect-or-pay-it
- **STR management**: full-service managers commonly charge **20%–30% of revenue** (range 10%–40% across models). Cleaning is usually separate and charged to the guest. https://www.redawning.com/pm/post/airbnb-property-management-cost
- **AirDNA 2026 (mid-year update, July 2026)**: U.S. occupancy forecast **57.4%** (≈ pre-COVID 57%). RevPAR **+2.9%**, driven almost entirely by rate (ADR). Supply growth **2.7%** (cut from 4.6%). A national ADR $ figure is not in the fetched text (UNVERIFIED). https://www.airdna.co/blog/u.s.-mid-year-outlook-report-2026
- **Seasonality**: strong in beach and ski markets. Model monthly occupancy rather than annual averages (general; UNVERIFIED specifics).
- **Regulation example: NYC Local Law 18** (enacted Jan 9, 2022; enforced **Sept 5, 2023**):
  - Hosts must register with the Office of Special Enforcement (OSE). The host must live in the unit and be present. Maximum **2 guests**. No whole-unit stays under 30 days.
  - Platforms can't process unregistered bookings. Fines run $100–$5,000.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Law_18_of_2022
  - About 23,000 listings before enforcement → **~3,500 registered by June 2026 (≈ −85%)**. https://www.credaily.com/?p=222425
  - Always check city permits, HOA bylaws, zoning, and caps before buying.

### STR tax rules
- **14-day rule / "Augusta rule" (§280A(g))**: if you use a dwelling as a home and rent it **fewer than 15 days** in a year, you **don't report the rent and can't deduct rental expenses**. https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc415
- **Vacation home / personal use**: a unit is used "as a home" if personal-use days exceed the **greater of 14 days or 10% of days rented at fair rental**.
  - If so, allocate expenses by days, and rental deductions are **limited to rental income** (excess carries forward).
  - Report fair-rental and personal days on Schedule E line 2.
  - Topic 415 (above); Schedule E instructions: https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040se
  - Example (CALC): 200 rental days → limit is max(14, 20) = **20 personal days**.
  - The detailed personal-use-day definition (family, below-market rent, repair days) is in Pub 527 and was not re-read this session. UNVERIFIED.
- **The "STR loophole"**:
  - Under Pub 925, an activity is **not a "rental activity"** if the **average period of customer use is ≤7 days** (or ≤30 days with significant personal services, or with extraordinary services). Reg. §1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii). https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925 ; https://weaver.com/resources/maximize-tax-benefits-by-using-the-short-term-rental-strategy/
  - The average is computed per property: total days rented ÷ number of stays. Example: 240 nights over 40 stays = 6.0 days (passes). Over 30 stays = 8.0 days (fails). https://www.redawning.com/pm/post/short-term-rental-tax-loophole-2026
  - The $25k allowance and REPS then don't apply. If the owner **materially participates** (Reg. §1.469-5T), losses are **nonpassive** and can offset W-2 income. Common tests:
    - Test 1: **>500 hours**.
    - Test 2: substantially all participation.
    - Test 3: **>100 hours and not less than any other individual**.
  - Tests 2–3 are comparative, so cleaners' and managers' hours can defeat them. (Pub 925; RedAwning)
  - Typical play: cost seg + 100% bonus on post-Jan-19-2025 acquisitions → a large year-1 loss.
  - Limits and risks:
    - §461(l) excess business loss cap for 2026: **$256,000 single / $512,000 MFJ** (Rev. Proc. 2025-32 per RedAwning).
    - Some states decouple from bonus depreciation.
    - Depreciation is a **deferral**: it is recaptured on sale.
    - Contemporaneous time logs are essential, as this area draws heavy audit attention.
- **Substantial services → Schedule C + SE tax** (15.3%): daily maid service, meals, concierge, or tours make it hotel-like. Cleaning between guests doesn't count. CCA 202151005. https://www.natptax.com/news-insights/blog/key-irs-guidelines-for-handling-short-term-rentals/ ; RedAwning (above)
- **Depreciation life for STRs**: some practitioners argue that units used mostly transiently (≤30 days) are 39-year nonresidential, not 27.5-year. UNVERIFIED; practitioners disagree. Flag "consult a CPA."
- **1099-K**: OBBBA **retroactively reinstated** the pre-ARPA threshold. Payment platforms file a 1099-K only if payments **exceed $20,000 AND exceed 200 transactions**. https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000
  - Rental income is taxable even without a 1099-K. This is a general rule, not stated on the fetched page.

### Worked example: gross bookings vs. real profit (CALC, illustrative assumptions)
Assumptions: $500k home ($100k land), $400k loan at 7.4%, ADR $300, 60% occupancy, average stay 3 nights, cleaning fee $150/stay, cleaner cost $130/turn. Federal 24% bracket. Ignores state tax and occupancy tax, which Airbnb passes through.
- Nights 219, stays 73. Rent $65,700 + cleaning fees $10,950 = **gross bookings $76,650**.
- Airbnb 15.5% (incl. on cleaning fees): −$11,881
- Cleaning cost: −$9,490
- Management at 25% of rent: −$16,425
- Utilities/internet/streaming at $400/mo: −$4,800
- Supplies/consumables: −$2,400
- STR insurance: −$3,000 (assumption)
- Property tax at 1%: −$5,000
- Maintenance at 1%: −$5,000
- Furnishing replacement reserve: −$2,000
- Software/permits: −$1,100
- **NOI ≈ $15,554 → 3.1% cap rate.**
- Year-1 interest −$29,474, so profit before depreciation ≈ **−$13,920**. Depreciation $400k/27.5 = −$14,545, so the **tax loss is ≈ −$28,465**.
- Cash flow after the full mortgage payment ($33,234/yr): **≈ −$17,700/yr**.
- **Self-managed variant**: NOI ≈ $31,979, cash flow ≈ **−$1,255/yr**, tax loss ≈ −$12,040.
  - If the loophole applies (avg stay ≤7 days + material participation), that loss saves ≈ $2,890 at 24%.
  - Adding a cost seg (20% of the building = $80k bonus) gives a loss of ≈ −$88,600, about **$21,300** of year-1 federal tax deferral, which is later recaptured.
- **All-cash variant** (managed): NOI $15,554, minus depreciation, gives taxable ≈ $1,009 and tax ≈ $242. That is ≈ **2.8% after-tax on ~$540k invested** (incl. ~$40k closing and furnishing).
- Takeaway: $76.6k of "bookings" can mean negative cash flow. Platform fees, cleaning, and management eat roughly 50% of gross before any mortgage, tax, or insurance cost.

## 6. Mortgage Basics

- **Fixed vs ARM**: a fixed rate never changes. An ARM (e.g., 5/6, 7/6) is fixed for an initial period, then resets with an index (SOFR) + margin, subject to caps. (Standard definitions; UNVERIFIED this session.)
- **Points**: 1 point = 1% of the loan, paid upfront to lower the rate. Break-even = cost ÷ monthly savings. The "~0.25% rate cut per point" figure is UNVERIFIED and lender-specific.
- **15 vs 30-yr (CALC, Oct 15, 2026 rates)**: on a $334k loan, 30-yr at 7.40% = $2,313/mo with **$498.5k** total interest. 15-yr at 6.71% = $2,948/mo with **$196.7k** total interest. Freddie Mac PMMS link above.
- **PMI (conventional, <20% down)**: you can **request** cancellation when the balance reaches **80% of original value** (good payment history, no junior liens, possibly an appraisal). It ends **automatically at 78%** of original value if you're current, and at the latest at the loan's **midpoint**. Covers loans closed on or after July 29, 1999. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/when-can-i-remove-private-mortgage-insurance-pmi-from-my-loan-en-202/
- **FHA MIP**:
  - Upfront **1.75%** of the loan.
  - Annual **0.15%–0.75%** (most pay **0.55%**) for loans endorsed on or after Mar 20, 2023.
  - With **<10% down** it lasts the **life of the loan** (refinance to remove). With ≥10% down it lasts **11 years**.
  https://www.nerdwallet.com/mortgages/learn/fha-mortgage-insurance
- **DTI limits (Fannie Mae B3-6-02)**: manual underwriting max **36%**, up to **45%** with credit score and reserves. Desktop Underwriter max **50%**. https://homebuyer.com/guidelines/fannie-mae/debt-to-income-ratios-b3-6-02
  - The **28/36 rule** (housing ≤28% of gross income, all debt ≤36%) is a traditional affordability guideline. UNVERIFIED this session.
  - The FHA/QM "43%" reference is UNVERIFIED; general QM moved to a price-based test.
- **Refinance break-even (CALC)**: $6,000 in costs ÷ $250/mo savings = **24 months**. Refinance only if you'll stay longer, and compare total interest when restarting a 30-yr term.
- **Assumable mortgages**: FHA, VA, and USDA loans are generally assumable with lender approval. Most conventional loans have due-on-sale clauses. Valuable when the existing rate is far below market (e.g., a 3% loan vs 7.4%). UNVERIFIED this session.

## 7. Smart Leverage Overall

- **"Good" vs "bad" leverage** (synthesis/judgment):
  - "Good" leverage is long-term, fixed-rate, non-callable, amortizing debt on an asset you use or that produces income (e.g., a 30-yr fixed mortgage). There are no margin calls, and inflation erodes the real payment. AWOCS calls a low fixed mortgage "a huge asset" (link in §3).
  - Avoid borrowing for consumption or depreciating assets.
- **Margin loans**:
  - Reg T lets you borrow up to **50%** of the purchase price. FINRA minimum maintenance equity is **25%**, and firms typically require **30–40%**.
  - The firm **may sell your securities without consulting you**, and you **can lose more than you invested**.
  - SEC example: buy $16k with $8k borrowed, and value falls to $12k. Equity of $4k passes 25% ($3k needed) but fails 40% ($4.8k needed → $800 call).
  https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-29
  - Forced-liquidation episodes in the 2008 crash and the Mar 2020 COVID crash are well known. Specific statistics are UNVERIFIED this session.
- **SBLOCs**:
  - **Non-purpose** loans: proceeds can't be used to buy securities.
  - **Interest-only**, **variable rate** (prime/SOFR + spread).
  - **Maintenance calls** must typically be met in 2–3 days, and the lender can sell pledged assets **without notice**, which can trigger taxable gains.
  - SBLOCs are **demand loans** that can be called at any time.
  https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/securities-backed-lines-credit
- **"Buy, borrow, die"**: borrow against appreciated assets instead of selling them, then heirs receive a **step-up in basis at death** (Pub 551, §2). Risks: rising variable rates, margin/maintenance calls in a crash, and interest compounding. Mostly relevant for large taxable portfolios.
- **401(k) loans**:
  - Limit is the **lesser of $50,000 or 50% of the vested balance**. Plans may allow up to $10k if 50% is less than $10k.
  - Repay within **5 years** with at least quarterly payments; a longer term is allowed for buying a primary residence.
  - A default is a **deemed distribution**: taxable, plus a possible 10% penalty.
  - If you leave your job, a loan offset can be rolled over by the **tax-return due date (incl. extensions)**.
  - Hidden costs: lost market returns, and repayment with after-tax dollars.
  https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-loans
- **Box-spread financing** (advanced): borrowing at near-Treasury rates by selling an options box spread. It requires options approval and margin and carries execution, early-assignment (use European-style index options), and tax complexity. Mention only as advanced. UNVERIFIED this session.
- **Leveraged/inverse ETFs**: they target **daily** returns, so holding longer than one day can diverge sharply ("volatility decay"), especially in volatile markets. SEC/FINRA examples:
  - Over 4 months, an index rose 2% while a 2x ETF **fell 6%**.
  - Over 2 days, an index fell 1% while a 2x ETF fell 4%.
  https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/sec-finra
- **Keep total leverage modest** (judgment, UNVERIFIED as a formal standard): track household **debt-to-assets** (e.g., under ~30–50% excluding a fixed mortgage on a long-held home), stay within DTI limits (§6), keep a 3–6 month emergency fund before investing borrowed money, and never use callable debt (margin, SBLOC, HELOC) for illiquid or long-horizon bets.
- **Pay off the mortgage early?** (CALC/framework): compare the **after-tax mortgage rate** with the **after-tax safe yield** (T-bills/CDs/HYSA).
  - Most people don't itemize (§2), so the after-tax mortgage cost is usually the full rate.
  - A **3%** mortgage vs a 4% safe yield taxed at 24% fed (+ state) ≈ 3.0% after-tax is roughly a wash. Keep the cheap loan and stay liquid.
  - A **7.4%** mortgage: prepaying is a guaranteed ~7.4% return, which beats safe after-tax yields.
  - Order of priorities: employer 401(k) match → high-interest debt → emergency fund → then compare.
  - Note: home equity is illiquid, and prepayments can't be pulled back out without a HELOC or refinance.
