## 5. Credit cards
- Typical foreign transaction fee ~3% (range 1%-3%; Amex standard 2.7%). https://www.nerdwallet.com/best/credit-cards/no-foreign-transaction-fee (updated Oct 1, 2026)
- No-FTF cards: Chase Sapphire Preferred, Chase Sapphire Reserve; ALL US-issued Capital One cards (Venture, Quicksilver, Savor...); Discover (all cards, but limited acceptance abroad); Wells Fargo Autograph/Autograph Journey; BofA Travel Rewards & Premium Rewards; Citi Strata Premier; Amex Platinum & Gold; US Bank Altitude Connect. Same source.
- Dynamic currency conversion: "usually a bad deal because the exchange rates are considerably worse" and doesn't avoid FTFs -- choose to pay in local currency. Same source.
- 0% balance-transfer cards: best offer >=15 months; listed range 15-21 months (e.g., Citi Diamond Preferred 21 mo, Wells Fargo Reflect 21 mo, U.S. Bank Shield 21 cycles, Citi Double Cash 18 mo, Chase Freedom Unlimited/Flex 15 mo). https://www.nerdwallet.com/best/credit-cards/balance-transfer (updated Oct 1, 2026)
- BT fee typically 3%-5%, usually $5 minimum (some 3% intro then 5%). Same source.
- Rules: generally can't transfer between cards of the same issuer; transfer windows vary -- 60 days (BankAmericard, Chase Freedom), 120 days (Wells Fargo Reflect), 4 months (Citi). Same source.
- Purchases: on BT-only promos (e.g., Citi Double Cash "Intro APR does not apply to purchases") new purchases can accrue interest -- pay off transfer before using the card for purchases. Same source.
- Deferred interest (store cards/medical financing) vs true 0%: deferred interest charges retroactive interest on the full original balance from day one if not paid in full by deadline; true 0% charges interest only on the remaining balance from the end date. Example: $5,000, $1,000 left -> deferred charges interest on $5,000; 0% intro on $1,000. https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/what-is-deferred-interest/ (Apr 15, 2026)
- CARD Act payment allocation (Reg Z 1026.53): amount above the minimum must go to the highest-APR balance first; exception -- in the last 2 billing cycles before a deferred-interest promo expires, excess goes to the deferred balance first. https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/53/
- Hard inquiry: usually <5 FICO points; on report up to 2 years, counts in FICO for 1 year; rate-shopping inquiries within 14 days (older FICO) / 45 days (newer) count as one; mortgage/auto/student-loan inquiries ignored for 30 days before scoring. https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/credit-reports/credit-checks-and-inquiries
- Card applications are not covered by the 30-day auto/mortgage/student shelter; whether FICO dedupes card inquiries -- UNVERIFIED (generally assume each card app counts). New accounts also lower average age of accounts (part of the 15% "length" factor).
- Utilization: impact gets more pronounced around 30%; "ideally in the single digits"; Experian Q3 2024 data -- 800-850 scorers average 7.1%, 740-799 average 15.2%. https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/credit-education/score-basics/credit-utilization-rate/
- FICO factor weights: payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score

## 6. Car buying

### Car loan interest deduction (OBBBA, IRC 163(h)(4)) -- CONFIRMED with corrections
- Tax years 2025-2028 (tax years beginning after 12/31/2024 and before 1/1/2029). https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/163 ; https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-tax-deductions-for-working-americans-and-seniors
- Cap $10,000 interest per year. Available to itemizers AND non-itemizers. Same sources. (Form used, e.g. Schedule 1-A: UNVERIFIED.)
- Phase-out: reduced by $200 per $1,000 (or part) of MAGI over $100,000 single / $200,000 joint -> a full $10,000 deduction is gone at $150,000 / $250,000 MAGI. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/163
- Loan: originated after 12/31/2024; for personal use; secured by a FIRST LIEN on the vehicle; refinance of a qualifying loan generally OK; leases, fleet, commercial, salvage/scrap excluded. Same sources.
- Vehicle: NEW (original use starts with you -- used cars don't qualify); car/minivan/van/SUV/pickup/motorcycle; GVWR <14,000 lbs; final assembly in the US (check dealer label or VIN plant code via NHTSA decoder). Same sources.
- Must report the VIN on the return each year claimed; lenders must file info returns showing interest. https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-tax-deductions-for-working-americans-and-seniors

### Loan vs. cash example (my calculation, using rates above)
- $35,000 new car, 60 months at 6.35% (Experian Q2 2026 new avg) -> payment $682.36; total interest $5,942 (yr1 $2,046, yr2 $1,644, yr3 $1,216, yr4 $760, yr5 $275).
- Alternative: pay cash, OR keep $35,000 in VMFXX at 3.83% and draw the payment monthly. After 22% federal tax the fund earns ~$2,474 over 5 years -> financing costs ~$3,468 MORE than cash (at 24% tax: ~$3,537 more).
- If deduction-eligible (new, US-assembled, MAGI <=$100k, 22% bracket): saves ~0.22 x $5,942 = ~$1,307 -> financing still ~$2,160 worse than cash. Rule: finance only if loan APR < your AFTER-TAX safe yield (e.g., 0%-2.9% promos), and watch for forfeited cash rebates.
- At 0% APR: payment $583.33, $0 interest -> financing wins by ~the after-tax MMF earnings (~$2,400).

### Depreciation -- sources CONFLICT; soften the copy
- Older Carfax-cited figure: ~20% first year, ~60% over 5 years. https://www.ramseysolutions.com/saving/car-depreciation (updated Jul 22, 2025)
- Current Carfax (Apr 23, 2026): >10% in first month, ~12.5% first year, ~5%/yr in years 2-5; average car worth ~66% of price after 5 years (~34% loss). https://www.carfax.com/blog/car-depreciation
- Suggested wording: "New cars commonly lose 10%-20% in year one and roughly 35%-60% over five years, depending on model and mileage."

### 20/4/10 rule
- Common rule of thumb: 20% down, loan <=4 years, total car costs (payment + insurance + fuel) <=10% of income (gross vs take-home varies by source) -- no authoritative source fetched; UNVERIFIED.
- Bankrate (Dec 16, 2025): total car costs <=20% of take-home pay; Edmunds: new-car payment <=15% of take-home, used <=10%. https://www.bankrate.com/loans/auto-loans/how-much-car-can-i-afford/

## 7. Emergency fund
- Rule of thumb: 3-6 months of living expenses; more for freelancers/seasonal/hard-to-replace jobs; start with ~$500 starter goal; keep in a separate high-yield savings account, not long-term investments. https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/banking/emergency-fund-why-it-matters (updated Oct 15, 2026)
- CFPB: amount depends on situation; keep it "safe, accessible," and not tempting to spend; bank/credit union accounts are "generally considered one of the safest places." https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/
- FDIC: insures at least $250,000 per depositor, per ownership category, per insured bank; money market DEPOSIT accounts are covered; mutual funds (incl. money market funds) and stocks are not. https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/understanding-deposit-insurance
- Options today: HYSA ~4.0%-4.27% (FDIC-insured); VMFXX 3.83% / VUSXX 3.90% (not FDIC, very low risk); 3-month T-bill 4.26% (federal-taxable, state-tax-exempt -- TreasuryDirect page not fetchable, exemption is well established). Sources in sections 3-4.
